STYLE DIRECTIVES: CENTURY-OLD PHOTOGRAPH
================================================================================

1. GLOBAL VISUAL FOUNDATION:
- Authentic damaged, aged, physically worn archival photograph.
- Tonal Range: Timeless warm faded sepia, aged albumen print tones, or rich silver gelatin monochrome with deep dynamic range.
- Edge & Border Treatment: Physically worn, irregular edges with naturally torn, chipped, and frayed cardstock borders.
- Surface Imperfections: Random black dust spots, photographic emulsion pitting, and thin vertical black hairline scratches.
- Subject Legibility: Central historical subject remains sharp, clear, and legible; physical wear is concentrated around edges and perimeter.
- STRICTLY FORBIDDEN: Modern vibrant RGB saturated colors (no modern bright blue skies or oceans, no digital greens, no modern color grading). Zero 3D rendering, zero cartoon elements.

2. HISTORICAL ACCURACY & HUMAN SUBJECTS:
- Natural, realistic human anatomy, period-appropriate hairstyles, facial hair (e.g. Victorian/Edwardian moustaches, muttonchops, or clean-shaven), authentic expressions.
- Realistic period-accurate clothing: coarse wool, heavy linen, tailored waistcoats, brass buttons, authentic military uniforms, leather work boots, period hats.
- Realistic historical tools, weapons, carriages, steam engines, and architecture matching the exact historical era.

3. COMPOSITION & LIGHTING:
- Authentic early 20th-century camera optics: slight natural vignetting at frame corners, natural depth of field, natural directional daylight or period gaslight/oil lantern illumination.
- Widescreen 16:9 framing capturing historical documentary scale.

4. THE 12 SPECIALIZED ARCHIVAL IMAGE TREATMENTS & DYNAMIC LAYOUTS:
To create museum-grade historical documentary storytelling, do NOT make every shot a plain centered photo. Instead, selectively apply these 12 specialized treatments based on the narrative context of the scene (or cycle them organically with subtle pacing). Maintain approximately 40-50% of scenes as clean widescreen archival photographic plates so treatments feel intentional, powerful, and cinematic:

1. The Woodblock / Engraving Plate Split:
   - Description in Visual prompt: Place the 4:6 vintage photograph on one side of the 16:9 frame, paired with an antique copperplate engraving, woodcut illustration, or hand-drawn architectural sketch of the same location or subject on the other side.
   - Best Context: Blueprints, early architectural origins, city foundations, or comparing ancient sketches with photographic reality.

2. The Accordion / Leporello Souvenir Folder:
   - Description in Visual prompt: Show the 4:6 vertical image as one panel of an unfolding 19th-century accordion-style souvenir postcard booklet, with adjacent panels peeking out at slight angles across the horizontal 16:9 frame.
   - Best Context: Expeditions, traveling across regions, tourist vistas, or passage of distance/time.

3. Silver Mirroring & Chemical Bleeding:
   - Description in Visual prompt: Add iridescent, metallic silver discoloration around the dark edges of the frame (common in aging silver gelatin prints).
   - Best Context: Dramatic night operations, deep shadowy scenes, dark interior scenes, or intense, somber moments.

4. Fold Creases & Crushed Paper Texture:
   - Description in Visual prompt: Add subtle cross-hatch folding lines or creases as if the photo was folded up and kept in a pocket or diary for decades.
   - Best Context: Personal memories, soldier letters, intimate portraits, secret plans, or years of struggle.

5. The Double-Exposure / Diptych Split:
   - Description in Visual prompt: Place two related 4:6 vertical photos side-by-side inside the 16:9 frame, set against an aged parchment or dark wooden desk background, showing a wide shot next to a close-up detail.
   - Best Context: Contrasting broad context with intimate focus (e.g. wide battlefield next to a soldier's hands/weapon, cause and effect).

6. The Polarized / Document Archive Layout:
   - Description in Visual prompt: Center a single 4:6 vertical photo tilted slightly on a textured surface (like old leather, a vintage map, or coarse linen), with paper clips, handwritten notes, or a magnifying glass overlapping the border in the extra horizontal space.
   - Best Context: Investigative evidence, confidential telegrams, treaties, espionage, forensic history.

7. The Cinematic Split-Screen Trio:
   - Description in Visual prompt: Place three vertical 4:6 frames staggered across the 16:9 canvas (one slightly overlapping the other), creating a dynamic collage effect for fast-paced historical narration.
   - Best Context: Fast-paced montages, simultaneous actions occurring in different places, multi-party events.

8. Textured Cinematic Blur Background:
   - Description in Visual prompt: Center your crisp 4:6 vertical image in the middle of the 16:9 frame, and use a heavily blurred, darkened, and desaturated version of the same image (or a matching vintage texture) to fill the left and right side wings.
   - Best Context: Focused single-character portraits or iconic centerpiece figures requiring dramatic cinematic depth.

9. Antique Map Overlay:
   - Description in Visual prompt: Place the historical photograph over a detailed antique geographical map of the relevant location, including compass roses, faded borders, geographical markings, old routes, and handwritten location annotations.
   - Best Context: Military troop marches, territorial campaigns, voyages, borders, or strategic travel routes.

10. Full Antique Engraving Plate:
   - Description in Visual prompt: The WHOLE 16:9 frame is an antique copperplate or steel engraving — fine black-ink cross-hatching, stippling, etched linework on aged cream paper. Black ink only: NO color, NO sepia wash, NO photographic look, NO torn paper edges.
   - Best Context: Founding moments, blueprints, architecture, ships, machinery — scenes best told as an engraving rather than a photo.
   - Difference from treatment 1 (Woodblock / Engraving Plate Split): That one pairs a photo with an engraving side by side; this one is the FULL frame as an engraving.

11. Full-Frame Authentic Archival Photograph:
   - Description in Visual prompt: The WHOLE frame is a genuine period photograph — silver gelatin or albumen print, warm sepia tones, film grain, dust spots, thin scratches, faded contrast, slight vignette. The photograph FILLS the frame edge-to-edge with NO torn edges and NO paper border.
   - Best Context: Key moments, iconic scenes, portraits where the photo itself should fill the screen.
   - Difference from the baseline: The baseline century-old plate has torn/chipped edges; this treatment has NONE.

12. Full Historical Map Plate:
   - Description in Visual prompt: The WHOLE frame is an aged parchment period map — hand-drawn coastlines, ornate compass rose, period place names, trade routes, battle lines, empire territories as they were at the time of the story. Place names and borders must be period-accurate (no modern names/borders where they did not exist); NO modern map look, NO satellite, NO modern roads; the map must show the geography the narration is talking about, never a decorative generic map; the still is held completely still, NEVER pan or zoom across the map.
   - Best Context: Marches, campaigns, voyages, territorial change.
   - Difference from treatment 9 (Antique Map Overlay): That one places a photo OVER a map; this one is the map itself filling the frame.
