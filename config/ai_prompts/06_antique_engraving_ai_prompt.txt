STYLE DIRECTIVES: ANTIQUE ENGRAVING STYLE
================================================================================

1. GLOBAL VISUAL FOUNDATION:
- Aesthetic: 18th and 19th-century copperplate, steelplate, and woodblock engraving, reminiscent of classic historical reference volumes, encyclopedias (e.g. Diderot's Encyclopedie), and archival treatises.
- Medium: Pure black ink line work, cross-hatching, contour hatching, and stippling on aged cream or ivory laid paper.
- Line Quality: Crisp, disciplined, variable-weight engraved lines defining form, depth, and shadow through density of parallel and crossed hatch marks.
- Color Palette: Strictly monochrome black ink on cream/parchment paper. NO color washes, NO modern RGB colors, NO photographic tones.

2. SUBJECT MATTER & PERIOD FIDELITY:
- Portraits, naval battles, historical skirmishes, architectural elevations, merchant sailing ships, early steam locomotives, industrial machinery, and bustling street scenes.
- Every figure, tool, and setting must be drawn as an engraver of the period would have captured it.
- Strict historical accuracy in period-appropriate clothing (linens, breeches, frock coats, tricorne hats, Victorian waistcoats), weaponry, and masonry.

3. STILLS TREATMENT & PACING:
- Stills are always held completely still with no zoom, no pan, and no simulated camera movement.
- If dynamic motion or location atmosphere is needed, use stock video via the `VIDEO of ... KEYWORDS ...` rule.

4. STRICT NEVER-DO LIST (NEGATIVE RULES):
- NEVER include modern objects (modern machinery, wristwatches, asphalt, power lines, modern plastics).
- NEVER include any color, colored ink, watercolor, or color washes.

- NEVER include torn paper edges or chipped frayed paper borders (torn edges belong exclusively to century-old photograph style, NOT to antique engraving).
