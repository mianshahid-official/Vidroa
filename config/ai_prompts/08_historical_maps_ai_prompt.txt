================================================================================
STYLE DIRECTIVES: HISTORICAL MAPS
================================================================================

1. GLOBAL VISUAL FOUNDATION:
- Aesthetic: Authentic aged parchment and vellum cartography, hand-drawn and hand-engraved maps from antiquity, the Age of Discovery, or 18th/19th-century military campaigns.
- Medium: Sepia, brown, and black hand-drawn ink linework on weathered, aged parchment with natural paper fiber texture, gentle tea-stained aging, and warm earthen tones.
- Cartographic Elements: Ornate nautical compass roses, rhumb lines, hand-lettered period typography (Gothic script, Latin Roman majuscules, or copperplate script), miniature hand-drawn mountain ranges, river systems, and illustrated sailing ships in sea expanses.

2. GEOGRAPHICAL RELEVANCE & HISTORICAL ACCURACY:
- Period-Accurate Place Names: Place names must be strictly period-accurate (e.g. Constantinople instead of Istanbul, Gaul instead of France, New Amsterdam instead of New York). Never use modern names where they did not exist.
- Period-Accurate Borders & Frontiers: Territorial outlines, imperial frontiers, and trade routes must depict political geography as it existed at the time of the story, not modern borders.
- Specific Narrative Geography: Every map must show the exact geography discussed in the narration (the specific strait, river crossing, military siege, or trade corridor). Never generate a generic, decorative, or disconnected map.

3. STILLS TREATMENT & MOTION RULE:
- Stills are held completely still at all times: strictly no pan, no zoom, no simulated Ken Burns movement across the map.
- If a scene needs motion, use real footage of the location through the normal `VIDEO of ... KEYWORDS ...` rule — never animate or pan across the map.

4. STRICT NEVER-DO LIST (NEGATIVE RULES):
- NEVER include modern map looks: no modern road networks, no highways, no Google Maps or GPS styling, no digital vector graphics.
- NEVER include modern country outlines or borders where they did not exist in the period.
- NEVER include modern place names, modern cities, or modern political subdivisions.
- NEVER include satellite imagery or aerial photographic maps.
- NEVER include decorative generic maps unrelated to the narration beat.
- NEVER pan or zoom across the map still (stills are always held still).
- NEVER include torn paper edges (torn edges belong exclusively to century-old photograph style).
- NEVER include modern bright saturated RGB colors or neon map markers.
